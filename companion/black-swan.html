Black swan entry tests

The decision that dominates every other modeling detail is the entry sequence: selling an inherited property and redeploying proceeds into risk assets at the top of a euphoric market versus being already invested through it. This page publishes only public-market reference anchors so the risk conversation can happen honestly. It contains no household figures and no personalized projection. A staging plan is insurance, not a return source — in a steady bull market, staying in cash for 24–36 months is usually a cost, and that cost should be admitted out loud before it is chosen.

3 · Decision tests, stated before the numbers arrive

  1. Write the pre-commitment first. Pick the staging rule, the tranche schedule, the acceleration trigger (e.g., a large market fall with a written percentage) and the “enough” end state — in writing, dated — before any appraisal or sale closes.
  2. Liquidity runway first. Stage only what is not needed for near-term housing, tax payments, or the San Francisco property’s obligations; staging is for risk capital, not for the mortgage.
  3. Same dates, same horizons. Compare immediate investing, 24-month staging and 36-month staging on identical transfer dates, taxes and horizons — never on different calendars.
  4. Admission of the base rate. Historically a majority of calendar years are positive; staging usually trails a prompt deployment in a bull year. Choose it knowing that, or choose the lump sum knowing the crash-year case.
  5. Annual Extreme-Scenario Review. Once a year, replay the plan against: 1929-type, 1973–74-type, 2000–02-type, 2008-type and 2020-type anchors above, plus one user-chosen stress size. Record the outcome in the decision journal.
