TWO BLANK WORKSHEETS · RULES AND ANCHORS ONLY · NO HOUSEHOLD FIGURES

The two tax questions that decide the answer, as blank worksheets.

Rent builds a recapture liability you only meet at sale; the estate builds a cash requirement you only meet at death. Both are checklists of law here, not numbers. A CPA fills them in privately.

How to use these two worksheets. They are empty on purpose. Each line names the rule, the official anchor, and the question a CPA or estate attorney has to answer. Fill them in privately, on paper or in a local copy; nothing here asks for, stores, or transmits a figure.
01 · DEPRECIATION AND RECAPTURE

The rent path creates a tax bill you cannot see until you sell

Every year of rental produces a deduction now and a recapture liability later. Both sides have to be in the same comparison or the rent path looks better than it is.

LineWhat the rule saysOfficial anchor
1. Value of the improvements alone at the applicable starting pointDepreciation applies to the building, never the land. Split the basis between land and improvements from the appraisal; the land share is not depreciable.IRS Pub 527 · IRS Pub 551
2. The correct starting value: inherited basis or carryover basisInherited property generally starts at date-of-death fair market value; a gift, a trust with carryover basis, or a non-qualifying transfer does not. The wrong starting value misstates every later year.IRS Pub 551 · Form 706
3. Residential rental recovery period and conventionResidential rental real property is recovered over 27.5 years, straight line, mid-month convention. Non-residential or mixed-use portions differ, which is why the allocation matters.Form 4562 · Pub 527
4. Placed-in-service date when the home converts to a rentalDepreciation starts when the property is ready and available for rent, not when the first tenant signs. Vacancy before that date is handled differently from vacancy after it.Pub 527: placed in service
5. Improvements capitalised, and repairs deductedA new roof, a system replacement or a structural repair is capitalised and recovered over time; routine maintenance is deducted. Misclassifying either side distorts current income and later recapture.Pub 527: repairs vs improvements
6. Depreciation actually allowed or allowable in every rental yearRecapture is computed on the greater of allowed or allowable, so a year that was missed on the return is still recaptured. Reconstruct it from returns, not memory.Pub 523: recapturing depreciation · Form 4797
7. Section 121 exclusion available, and the depreciation carve-out inside itIf the home later qualifies as a main home, the exclusion can still apply — but the depreciation taken after 1997 is not sheltered by it and is recaptured separately.Topic 701 · Pub 523
8. Rate on unrecaptured section 1250 gainUnrecaptured section 1250 gain is taxed at a maximum 25% federal rate, separate from the 0/15/20% long-term capital-gain brackets, and California does not give it preferential treatment.Topic 409 · FTB: no CA preference
9. State treatment of the recapture and of the remaining gainCalifornia conforms to the federal depreciation mechanics but has no capital-gain rate preference, so the state cost of a sale can be materially higher than a federal-only estimate suggests.FTB capital gains · 2025 Form 540
10. Suspended or passive losses released on salePassive losses suspended in earlier years are generally released when the property is fully disposed of. That can offset part of the gain — or none of it, if the activity was never passive.Pub 527 · Form 4797
11. The 1% California surtax on taxable income above $1,000,000A sale can push a single year over the threshold. The surtax applies to taxable income above the threshold, not to the gain as such, so the rest of the year's income matters.RTC 17043 · Form 540 line 62
12. Net investment income tax, where it appliesA 3.8% surtax can apply to net investment income above the statutory threshold. Whether a particular sale is inside it depends on the year's income, not on the property.Topic 409: NIIT
02 · ESTATE LIQUIDITY AND PORTABILITY

A tax bill is a cash problem before it is a tax problem

The estate question is asked once, at death, across everything. The liquidity question is asked on the same day, and it is the one that forces sales.

LineWhat the rule saysOfficial anchor
1. Aggregate gross estate, not just these two propertiesThe estate tax question is asked at the level of the whole estate, including life insurance, retirement accounts, joint property, prior taxable gifts and business interests. Two houses alone do not answer it.Form 706 instructions · IRS estate tax
2. Whether a return is required at allFor 2026 the basic exclusion is $15,000,000 per person. A return is required when the gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts plus specific exemption exceeds that amount — but a return may still be worth filing to elect portability.Form 706: who must file · IRS 2026 amount
3. The nine-month clock, and the automatic six-month extensionA Form 706 is due nine months after the date of death. Form 4768 carries an automatic six-month extension to file; an extension of time to pay under section 6161 is a separate, discretionary request on the same form.Form 706: when to file · Form 4768
4. Portability: what it is, and why a timely return mattersPortability transfers a deceased spouse's unused exclusion to the survivor, but only if the estate elects it on a timely filed return. The election is not automatic.Form 706: portability election
5. Late portability, and its limitsA surviving spouse may be able to obtain portability after the deadline under the revenue procedure that allows a late election up to the fifth anniversary of death, subject to its conditions.Form 706: late portability
6. Liquidity: which assets can actually pay a tax billA house cannot pay an estate tax bill without being sold, borrowed against, or distributed in kind. Cash, marketable securities and life insurance are liquid; real property is not.IRS estate tax · Investor.gov
7. Deferral and installment options, and their eligibility conditionsClosely held business interests can qualify for installment payment of estate tax over an extended period. Whether a rental property qualifies depends on the estate's composition, not on the owner's preference.Form 706: section 6166
8. Deductions and credits that reduce the billAdministration expenses, debts, charitable bequests, the marital deduction and state death-tax credits all change the number. California has no estate tax, but other states may.Form 706: deductions · CA State Controller
9. Basis consistency between the return and the heirsHeirs must use a basis consistent with the value reported on the estate return, and the executor must furnish the statement. An appraisal that is never filed is not automatically the basis.Pub 551: consistent basis · Form 706
10. A reserve policy, written before the tax is dueThe plan should state how much cash or marketable securities stays liquid, who can authorise a sale, and what happens if a sale is needed in a bad market. That is a decision, not a projection.Investor.gov · Treasury bills
03 · WHAT THESE WORKSHEETS DO NOT DO

No number, no projection, no ranking

A worksheet is a checklist of rules, not a tax computation. The arithmetic depends on facts that are not public and must not be.

LimitationWhy it is deliberateWhat would close it
No dollar figures are publishedAny household value, even rounded, is traceable through county records. Publishing it would breach the repository's privacy policy.A CPA completes the worksheet privately and returns a reviewed estimate. See also Pub 551 and flagged items.
No depreciation schedule is assumedThe recovery period, the placed-in-service date and the improvement/land split all change the answer, and all three are facts about one property.Pub 527 · an appraisal that splits land from improvements
No estate-tax liability is estimatedThe 2026 exclusion is $15,000,000 per person, which is an anchor, not a forecast of the law at the relevant date, and not a statement about this estate.Year-of-death Form 706 instructions
No state estate tax is assumedCalifornia requires no estate return for deaths after 2004 under current law; residence, situs of property, and future law can change that.CA State Controller
No portability outcome is predictedPortability depends on a timely election, a surviving spouse, and the deceased spouse's remaining exclusion — all facts outside a public page.Form 706: portability
04 · ORDER OF OPERATIONS

Do these in this order, not in the order they become urgent

Most expensive mistakes in an inherited-property decision come from doing the arithmetic before the facts, or the sale before the tax.

StepActionWhy it comes hereAnchor
1Fix the date-of-death value and the land/improvement splitIt sets basis, the estate return, the Prop 19 calculation and every later gain figure. Everything downstream moves if this is wrong.Pub 551
2Determine whether a Form 706 is required or merely advisableThe nine-month clock starts at death, not at the decision. Portability can be lost by inaction.Form 706
3Build the household's liquidity need before deciding on the houseThe reserve requirement can rule out a hold path regardless of its projected return.Investor.gov
4Compute the rent path with depreciation and recapture in the same columnA pre-tax rent comparison systematically overstates the rent path.Pub 527
5Only then compare the paths on identical datesWith the first four settled, the comparison page becomes usable rather than decorative.Comparison · Form 706
