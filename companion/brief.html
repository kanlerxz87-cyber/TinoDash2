Attorney & CPA Briefing Sheet

One page. Facts as they stand, the nine gates that decide the answer, the eight paths being compared, and the exact questions for each professional.

Facts as they stand

  • Property A: Inherited single-family home in Santa Clara County, California. Expected to transfer by trust in a future period; the exact date is unknown. No value, address or parcel number is printed here — rounded planning figures are on the figures page.
  • Property B: San Francisco multi-unit building already owned by the prospective heir; partly rented, partly owner-occupied. No value, address, rent or unit-level terms are printed here.
  • The distinction that matters: Property B is already owned and its property-tax base is established. Property A arrives with a date-of-death basis and a change-of-ownership assessment question. They must never be netted into one number.
  • Objective: Choose between living in, renting, selling, splitting, staging or deferring, on after-tax, risk-adjusted grounds, with the reasoning written down before the figures arrive.

The nine gates

  • G1 Eligible transferee and principal-residence status — RTC 63.2
  • G2 Occupancy within one year and exemption filed — RTC 63.2(a)(1)(B)
  • G3 Date-of-death fair market value and land/improvement split — IRS Pub 551
  • G4 Estate size, prior taxable gifts, portability — Form 706
  • G5 Parcel tax rate area and published rate — SCC rate book
  • G6 Evidenced market rent — Rent rules
  • G7 Insurance quotes and insurability — CA Dept. of Insurance
  • G8 Five-year capital plan — CSLB
  • G9 Same-tax-year sale computation and exchange decision — RTC 17043 · 1031

Questions for the attorney

  • Confirm the eligible-transferee relationship, the principal-residence history, and the trust’s authority over occupancy, sale and distribution.
  • State the one-year occupancy and exemption-filing obligations in writing, with the date they start.
  • Confirm what the trust says about who may live there, for how long, and who pays for what.
  • Advise on any exchange, sale restriction or beneficiary conflict before a listing is signed.
  • Advise on estate liquidity if the estate may exceed the federal exclusion, and on the portability election.
  • Review the San Francisco leases, lawful unit status and owner move-in consequences.

Questions for the CPA

  • Establish date-of-death basis and the consistent-basis statement; reconcile it to the estate return.
  • Compute a same-tax-year sale first: federal gain, California gain, and the 1% additional tax above $1,000,000 of taxable income.
  • Estimate the reassessed property tax under both Prop 19 outcomes, using the parcel’s actual tax rate area.
  • Rebuild depreciation allowed or allowable on the San Francisco property and model recapture on any sale.
  • Model the rent path net of depreciation, and the living-then-selling path with Section 121 only if the tests are met.
  • Price the transfer tax, recording fees and closing costs for the actual sale scenario.

What the evidence already says about staged investing

Replaying 1928–2025, parking sale proceeds in Treasury bills for 24 months and then phasing into equities over 36 months beat immediate investing in 15.7% of ten-year windows, with a median shortfall of 74.9 points of starting value. It won decisively in a 1929 entry (+107.9 points) and lost in a 2020 entry (−60.1 points). Staging is a deliberate purchase of crash protection, not a free improvement, and should be pre-committed in writing or not used at all.
