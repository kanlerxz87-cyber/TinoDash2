Attorney & CPA Briefing Sheet

Objective: choose among living in, renting, selling, staged reinvestment, and mixed paths for an inherited home (Property A, expected by trust 2040–2050), while an already-owned three-unit home (Property B) is modeled separately. All values rounded to the nearest $10; full-precision records stay private.

Known facts (rounded)

  • Property A (Santa Clara County city): planning value $3,100,000 (2026); assessed $550,540; tax $7,210 (2025); solar net credit; water $100–150/mo; insurance guess $1,500 (quote pending).
  • Property B (San Francisco): value $1,800,000; assessed $114,250; tax $2,440 (2025); rent $7,150/mo (2 of 3 units); owner can occupy without losing rent; Prop H parcel tax $250/yr from FY2027–28.
  • Federal estate exclusion $15,000,000 (2026, PL 119-21, indexed; top rate 40%). California: no estate tax (current law).
  • Prop 19 family-home allowance $1,044,586 (transfers Feb 16, 2025–Feb 15, 2027; RTC 63.2); SCC typical TRA 1.1488% + ~$1,010 fixed charges.
  • Treasury curve Nov 24, 2026: 3M 4.36%, 1Y 4.59%, 2Y 4.63% (phased-path parking leg).
  • Transfer taxes: SCC $0.55/$500 (no A-city tax); SF 0.75% at $1M–$5M (cliff brackets).

Questions for the attorney

  • Confirm eligible-transferee status and that Property A was the transferor's principal residence (RTC 63.2).
  • Trust powers/timing; sale authority; debt; any constraints on sale or exchange.
  • Prop 19 filing windows: homeowners' exemption within 1 year; exclusion claim within 3 years — confirm strategy only if long-term occupancy is genuine.
  • Form 706: required? Portability election? Prior taxable gifts (Form 709 history)? Rev. Proc. 2022-32 late-election viability.
  • Property B: legal unit count, leases, Rent Board/AB 1482 posture, owner-move-in consequences.

Questions for the CPA

  • Date-of-death valuation and consistent-basis statements (IRC 6035); model the same-tax-year sale first (gain ≈ $0).
  • Federal/California sale tax: LTCG 0/15/20% + 3.8% NIIT; CA ordinary rates; Form 540 line 62 = 1% of CA taxable income above $1,000,000 (2025 label: Behavioral Health Services Tax).
  • IRC 121 ($250K/$500K) for any living-then-sell path; depreciation and recapture (Pub 544) — rebuild B's depreciation schedule.
  • Reassessed property tax by actual TRA; Prop 19 outcome; supplemental bill year 1.
  • Estate liquidity: fund any estate tax within the filing window; coordinate §1031 if an exchange is wanted before listing.

Model outputs (base case, 2045 transfer, 30y, nominal)

  • Ranking: 1) Sell + invest $54.4M; 2) Sell + phased $51.4M; 3) Live-in (Prop 19) $16.6M; 4) Rent $15.4M.
  • Phased vs lump-sum: no-crash cost 4.5–6.6% of terminal; year-1 −50% crash: phased ahead by +$12.7M–$15.6M; 1929-style −89%: ahead by up to +$30.5M.
  • Property tax at 2045: lived-in ≈ $54,700 (Prop 19); rented ≈ $69,480 (no exclusion) vs $7,210 today.
  • Net sale proceeds 2045 ≈ $5,646,000 (after 5% commission, $6,560 transfer tax, fees).
  • Live-in annual gap vs sell-and-invest ≈ $203,250/yr (2045 basis: projected rent/insurance/carrying costs, expected return difference, pre-tax).

Decision criteria (agreed before the figures)

  • Prop 19 eligibility + genuine occupancy → live-in viable; otherwise exclude.
  • Verified market rent > ≈$10,700/mo (2045 terms) → rent path competitive.
  • Estate above projected exclusion → liquidity plan + portability first.
  • Hidden capex > ~2% of value → favor earlier sale.
  • Crash in first 1–2 years (if knowable) → phased entry wins decisively.

Open items the household must still supply

  • Written valuation of A at date of death; rental comps for A; insurance quotes (both); B's depreciation schedule; estate inventory incl. other cash; five-year inspection/bid reports.
  • Household statements: occupancy intent, risk tolerance (phase window), liquidity floor.
Decision guardrail: the criteria above were fixed before the final figures were run, so the outcome is not reverse-engineered from one projection.
