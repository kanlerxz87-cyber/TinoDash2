EIGHT PATHS · ONE CALENDAR · RISK SHOWN BESIDE RETURN

Compare all choices at the same dates, with the risks attached.

Living, renting, selling, splitting, staging, deferring — measured at one shared start and one shared end, with taxes, costs, occupancy, capital costs and liquidity stated for each. Mixed paths sit beside the pure ones because the honest answer is often a mixture.

Everything on this page is a protocol, not a projection. No value, rent or tax figure for either property is published anywhere on this site. The comparison defines what has to be measured, on what dates, and what is deliberately left out; the calculator performs the arithmetic only after you supply private inputs in your own browser.

02 · REINVESTMENT RISK

What the staged path costs, next to what it buys

Every headline about lower crash-entry risk sits beside the price of holding cash.

Reinvestment riskWhat it isWhat the evidence showsWhere it is modelled
Cash drag during the parking windowTreasury bills returned roughly 0–14% nominal in the historical record, while equities compounded far more over the same decades. Every month parked is a month of forgone equity exposure.Median ten-year outcome of the 24-month park + 36-month phase-in was −74.9 points of starting value versus immediate investing.Crash replay · Treasury rates
Reinvestment risk at each maturityA bill ladder resets at unknown future yields. If short rates fall, later tranches lock in lower returns; if they rise, the ladder is a benefit.Modelled as three cases: 0% while parked, historical bill returns, and (as an upper bound) the equity return on idle cash. The ranking did not flip.Sensitivity · Daily Treasury rates
Delay to full exposureThe plan is not fully invested until the parking and phase-in months have elapsed, so it misses any early recovery by construction.A 2008 entry at ten years finished behind: phasing −24.2 points. A 2020 entry finished well behind because the recovery was immediate.Episodes · NBER dates
The protection is real but narrowPhasing only wins when the crash arrives early in the window. That is exactly the scenario it is bought for — and the reason it must be pre-committed rather than improvised.Best historical window: 1929 entry, +107.9 points at ten years. Wins overall: 15.7% of ten-year windows.Crash replay
Behavioural risk in both directionsA rule that is abandoned during a drawdown is worse than either pure policy, because it converts a plan into a reaction.Write the tranche dates, the acceleration trigger and the end date before the sale closes.Pre-commitment · Investor.gov
