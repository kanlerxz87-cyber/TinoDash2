1 · Inherited home: live

Model actual occupancy cost vs. rent forgone elsewhere and Prop 19 eligibility only after assessor confirms facts. Add utilities, insurance, tax, maintenance and financing.

2 · Inherited home: rent

Model market rent less vacancy, management, insurance, repairs, capital reserve, tax, debt service and income tax. Prop 19 occupancy eligibility is generally not available if not occupied as required.

3 · Inherited home: sell now

Same-year sale is the first comparison. Model date-of-death basis, closing/transfer/recording costs, tax, debt payoff, liquidity and a documented investment policy.

4 · Live, then sell

Vary move-in date, qualifying occupancy duration, sale year and state/federal principal-residence tests. Tax benefit is not automatic; income-producing portions may be treated differently.

5 · Rent, then sell / 1031 alternative

Track depreciation allowed/allowable, recapture and disposition tax. Ask whether a 1031 exchange is actually desired before sale; it is limited to qualifying business/investment real property—not a purely personal residence.

6 · Mixed household path

Keep the existing SF property, retain permitted tenancy income, use its vacant unit if lawful, and choose a separate path for the inherited home. Also test selling one, not both, and keeping an estate-liquidity reserve.

Black-swan path · phased investing after sale

Stress-test an illustrative ladder that parks proceeds in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries for 24–36 months after inheritance, then phases into equities over a separately selected 2- or 3-year period. This is an experiment, not a safe return or a prediction that markets will repeat history. A ladder limits early equity exposure but adds cash drag, inflation and reinvestment risk: maturing securities may roll at lower yields. Staging does not eliminate market losses.

Black-swan economic regimes · historical context, not a forecast

Long horizons should not assume smooth growth or stable inflation. History shows different stress mechanisms, not one repeatable “crash percentage.” The National Bureau of Economic Research dates the Great Depression contraction from an August 1929 peak to a March 1933 trough; it dates the Great Recession from a December 2007 peak to a June 2009 trough. These were multi-year macroeconomic contractions; recession dates are not stock-return paths.

Deep credit / deflation shock

Stress delayed recovery, falling prices/income, unemployment, credit tightening and multi-year equity weakness; test a second leg down after a partial recovery. Keep the Treasury hold and spending reserve separate from equities.

Financial-system / housing shock

Stress simultaneous market drawdown, financing/liquidity stress, weaker rents, vacancy, property value pressure and elevated sale friction. Re-run shock timing before, during and after the 24–36 month Treasury hold; compare missed rebound too.

Stagflation / rate-reset shock

Stress persistent inflation, rising replacement/insurance/repair costs and Treasury yields that reset lower or higher at maturity. Cash yield may lag inflation; long Treasury prices can move with rates. Use CPI history as context, not a future inflation estimate.

What this tool actually tests: an entered single equity shock with constant annual return/yield assumptions. It does not reconstruct either episode, model inflation/credit/property correlations, or estimate probabilities. For a real replay, use a consistent total-return/price series and Treasury and CPI data, state the index and reinvestment assumptions, and have an adviser validate source data.

50-year uncertainty: do not extrapolate one smooth line

Multi-decade projections are especially fragile because inflation, real returns, rates, property costs, law, insurance availability, climate/fire risk, household health and rental rules change over time. Keep nominal dollars and today’s dollars separate. Use low/base/high ranges chosen with independent advisers—not one assumed compound-growth rate.

  • Property value: appraisal/comps and multiple appreciation paths; no guaranteed annual increase.
  • Tax assessment: current base year, transfer/reassessment events, Prop 13 cap, Prop 8 review and local levies.
  • Costs: insurance renewal quotes, utility statements, inspection, deferred maintenance and five-year capital schedule.
  • Investments: net returns after fees/taxes/inflation, volatility, drawdowns, sequence and withdrawal needs.
  • Annual rebuild: update rules, amounts, facts and household priorities; preserve dated versions.

Official rules that inform the decision

Inherited basis is generally fair market value at death (or alternate valuation in qualifying cases); obtain and retain a qualified written appraisal. California’s principal-residence gain exclusion must meet applicable ownership/use tests; inheritance alone does not make a sale tax-free. Federal 1031 exchanges concern qualifying business or investment real property, not property held solely as a personal residence. See IRS Pub. 551, Pub. 523, and Form 8824 instructions.
