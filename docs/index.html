The Cupertino Inheritance — What to Do With “Property A”

Heir already owns a San Francisco rental triplex (“Property B”) outright and can live in it without losing rental income. A separate Cupertino home (“Property A”, 95014, ≈ $3,100,000 in Nov 2026) is expected to be inherited by trust around 2040–2050. This desk models the three pure paths — live in it, rent it, sell and reinvest — plus mixed paths such as Black-Swan-staged entry into stocks (24–36 months in 3M–2Y Treasuries, then phase in), and reports the reasoning with taxes, debt, occupancy, capital costs and liquidity side by side.

READ FIRST — figures are planning estimates. Every dollar figure is rounded to the nearest $10 and is a model output or a public average, never a quote. The Flagged for Review page lists what is unverified (e.g., Property B’s implied ≈2.1% composite tax rate, the missing 2026–27 Property A tax bill, the Tax Rate Area lookup, SF transfer-tax churn). Do not sign anything until the pre-commitment conditions on the Investment Policy page are met in writing and the attorney/CPA sign the Briefing Sheet.

First screen: what would change the answer

Things that flip the recommendation

  1. An independent appraisal of Property A materially below the $3,100,000 estimate (or rent comparables above ~$10,000/mo) — re-ranks every path.
  2. Heirs want to occupy Property A ≥ 2 years. Prop 19 (primary residence, 1-year occupancy, cap ≈ factored base + $1,044,586, Feb 2025–Feb 2027 window) saves ≈ $43,500/yr vs full reassessment (≈ $26,950 vs ≈ $71,400 at 2040 FMV) — and §121’s $250,000/$500,000 exclusion can shelter later gains on post-inheritance appreciation.
  3. The inheritance year equals the sale year (“sell same tax year as death”) — the step-up basis still wipes pre-death gains; timing changes only the year, not the amount. Confirm with CPA.
  4. Estate exceeds ≈ $20,900,000 at the 2040 death date (OBBBA $15.0M 2026 exclusion, indexed ≈ 2.4%/yr → ≈ $20,900,000 in 2040, ≈ $26,500,000 in 2050): Form 706 due 9 months after death; portability election; 40% applies only above the exclusion.
  5. A 1929/1973/2000/2008-type drawdown at the reinvestment date — the staged-entry path (Treasuries first, phase in over 24–36 months) dominates lump sum in crashes and ties in calm markets.
  6. CA changes the capital-gains or property-tax regime (e.g., a revival of a state transfer tax or Prop 13 overhaul) — tracked on Sources, enacted vs proposed.
  7. Trust terms name a different distribution or sale authority than assumed (successor trustee powers, withholding, spousal/QTIP provisions).
  8. Debt/liens or occupancy changes (reverse mortgage, co-heir occupancy) — flips liquidity math.

Headline vs reinvestment risk paired

Model headline (baseline 4% property growth → inheritance 2040) Sell Property A ≈ $5,080,000 net at death; staged into stocks over 24–36 months and held 25 years at 7% ≈ $21,800,000–$34,800,000 (6–8% CAGR)

Reinvestment risk attached: the headline assumes an average equity return. A 1929-style entry (−86% DJIA peak→trough) turns $5,080,000 into ≈ $710,000 if fully lump-sum invested at the peak; 2008 (−55% peak→trough) into ≈ $2,290,000; 2000–02 dot-com (−49%) into ≈ $2,590,000. Staging in 3M–2Y Treasuries (≈ 4.3–4.9% in Nov 2026) for 24–36 months with monthly phased buys cuts expected entry risk roughly in half and is modeled on Mixed Paths and Black Swan Lab.

Executive summary (the whole decision in one screen)

1 · Recommendation (provisional)

Sell Property A; stage proceeds into Treasuries for 24–36 months; phase into a diversified equity index; keep Property B as-is. Living in A is the only path with a strong non-financial case; renting A is dominated on current law (Prop 19 denies the low assessed base to non-occupant heirs) — treat letting as a floor, not a plan.

2 · Why (economics)

Selling nets ≈ $5,080,000 tax-free at step-up (gain = post-inheritance appreciation only). Living costs ≈ $28,000/yr tax + ≈ $31,000/yr owner costs and forgoes ≈ $360,000/yr of portfolio return. Renting nets only ≈ $51,000/yr NOI after reassessment (≈ 1.0% unlevered yield) — far below a T-bill ladder, before depreciation recapture at sale.

3 · Why (taxes)

Estate tax: $0 expected if estate < ≈ $20,900,000 (2040, projected) — verify cash amount & portability. Income-tax basis: step-up erases pre-death gains; sell near death ⇒ ≈ $0 tax. Property tax: no Prop 19 ⇒ reassess ≈ $71,400/yr; occupied ⇒ ≈ $26,950/yr (cap + $1,044,586 allowance). CA: no estate/inheritance tax; 1% MHSA (Form 540 line 62) on CA taxable income > $1,000,000.

4 · Conditions (pre-commitment)

Sell path is chosen today, in writing, if: appraisal ≥ $4,700,000; no heir committed to occupancy ≥ 2y; staged-entry policy signed; estate liquidity confirmed for any Form 706 exposure. If occupancy commitment is genuine, switch to live-then-sell (2–5 yr), capture Prop 19 + §121 ($250,000/$500,000), then sell. Never chosen: rent-as-primary-plan unless reassessment math changes.

5 · What costs to expect to hold A

Water ≈ $120–$150/mo; electricity ≈ $0 net (solar credit ≈ +$10–$20/mo); insurance ≈ $1,600–$3,000/yr (owner HO-3; FAIR Plan fallback higher) — see Property A. SF side: water ≈ $330–$400/mo; electricity ≈ $30–$70/mo; landlord insurance ≈ $1,500–$2,000/yr; Prop H parcel tax ≈ $249/yr (from FY2027-28).

6 · What we still don’t know

Cash-inheritance amount; trust terms; debts/liens; co-heirs; appraisal; TRA & 2026 tax; 2027 exclusion indexing; CA/federal law changes; market level at 2040. Every gap and how to close it: Open Questions — 30+ items with owners and sources.

Cost of being wrong (why we plan before 2040)

Wrong on Prop 19

Miss the 1-year occupancy window or file the exclusion claim late ⇒ full reassessment to ≈ $5,370,000 ⇒ ≈ $71,400/yr instead of ≈ $26,950/yr — ≈ $44,500/yr, ≈ $1,560,000 over 35 years, plus retroactive bills.

Wrong on estate tax

If the estate exceeds ≈ $20,900,000 (2040) and nobody plans (portability, gifting, valuation discounts, irrevocable trusts), 40% on the excess — e.g., $5,000,000 over ⇒ $2,000,000 avoidable. Form 706 due 9 months after death.

Wrong on entry timing

Lump-sum into a 1929-style tape at 2040 peak ⇒ ≈ $710,000 terminal vs ≈ $3,300,000+ had the same dollars entered after the crash via 24–36-month staging (see Black Swan Lab).
