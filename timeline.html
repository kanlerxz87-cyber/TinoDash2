Process & deadline control

An inheritance is a sequence, not one decision day.

The date of death, trust distribution date, title transfer and sale date may differ. Calendar actual due dates with the estate attorney/CPA. The timeline below gives official deadlines only where a primary source verifies one; all entries require confirmation for this estate.

Protect liquidity first. Before selling, moving in or investing, identify estate debts, administration costs, property carrying costs, required tax payments and a reserve. Keep documentation of date-of-death value, ownership and all elections. Avoid letting a discretionary decision cause a missed statutory claim deadline.

Step-by-step timeline

Before the expected transfer (planning now through 2040–2050)

Agree in writing on household priorities and trigger conditions for live, rent, sell and mixed options. Prepare private estate inventory; ask about portability/prior taxable gifts; review trust and title; collect independently supported values, leases, insurance and capital plans. Reconfirm law annually. No inheritance date or amounts are assumed here.

Questions to resolve ↗ · IRS estate planning / filing reference ↗

Date of death · promptly

Secure property and records, notify trustee/insurers, arrange utilities and safety, document condition, preserve leases, obtain an independent written date-of-death appraisal. Identify transfer and ownership form, estate liquidity, debts, and professional decision-makers. Keep estate basis separate from property-tax assessment.

IRS inherited-basis rules ↗

By 9 months after death · federal estate-tax return checkpoint

Form 706 is generally due within nine months if required. A timely return can also be required to elect portability. Ask the CPA/attorney immediately whether the estate must file or should file for portability; do not assume the estate is below the filing threshold. A filing extension is not a reason to defer tax/liquidity planning.

IRS Form 706 / portability FAQ ↗

Within 1 year of qualifying family-home transfer · Prop 19 occupancy checkpoint

If relying on the parent-child / eligible family-home exclusion, the transferee generally must make the home their principal residence within one year and timely file the required claim/exemption. Confirm kinship, transferor’s prior principal residence, trust transfer date, valuation limit and assessor procedure with the county before the deadline. A rent-first plan can conflict with qualifying occupancy.

California BOE Prop 19 guide and claim checklist ↗

At distribution / change in title · before disposition

Get county assessor guidance on change in ownership, Prop 19 claim, supplemental assessment and appeal deadlines. Verify local Tax Rate Area/rate and all special charges from official bill. Obtain an estate basis statement and date-of-death valuation; line up insurance appropriate to occupancy/tenancy and keep sufficient cash.

BOE property tax assessment overview ↗ · Santa Clara County Assessor ↗

Before choosing sale, rental or investment path

Obtain same-date net sale estimates, written rental comparables, lease review, inspection/capital plan, insurance quotes and separate federal/California tax projections. Test a same-tax-year sale, living-then-sale, rent-then-sale and mixed paths. Decide whether a 1031 exchange is wanted before selling; get qualified advice and timing counsel.

IRS Section 1031 instructions ↗ · IRS home-sale exclusion guide ↗

Each year and after any input/law change

Refresh values, rent and insurance, tax law, assessment, leases, property conditions, financing and capital plan. Rebuild projections using the same transfer date and horizon. Record the model version, assumptions, source date and whether the decision changed. Review extreme scenarios annually.

Estate administration checklist

  • Identify acting trustee/executor, trust terms, beneficiaries and deadlines.
  • Inventory real estate, cash, securities, retirement/insurance assets, debts, lifetime gifts, business interests and ownership fractions privately.
  • Ask CPA/attorney about gross-estate calculation, federal exclusion for the year of death, portability, GST and any state/local tax or filing.
  • Ask whether Form 706 is needed to report estate tax and/or preserve a surviving spouse’s DSUE portability; document filing decision.
  • Obtain appraisal(s) at date of death and determine tax basis separately for each asset and ownership interest.
  • Secure estate cash for taxes, debts, repairs, insurance, property operations and distribution before discretionary investing.
  • For Prop 19, verify eligibility, one-year occupancy window, claim submission and assessor receipt in writing.
  • Track deeds, county notices, supplemental assessments, property tax due dates, insurance endorsements and appeal windows.
  • Before a sale, reconcile basis, depreciation, principal-residence use, rental allocation, expenses, liens, transfer tax and estimated tax.
  • Keep a dated decision record and a copy of the model inputs/output in the private estate file.

Record dates precisely

Store date of death, date trust/beneficiary acquires beneficial ownership, date legal title changes, date of Prop 19 claim, date first occupied, date rental begins/ends, date property listed/closed, tax year of sale and tax-return due dates. A trust distribution date is not automatically the controlling date for every tax or assessment rule.

Use an attorney-maintained calendar; statutory periods, mailing/filing methods, extensions and local assessor procedures must be independently confirmed for the actual case.
