01 · EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

What the figures say so far

Read before any table. Findings are conditional on the assumptions in Section 02 and change if a flagged input is wrong.

  1. The same-year sale is the baseline, and it is very hard to beat on simplicity. Selling in the tax year of the inheritance (basis = date-of-death value) leaves essentially no capital gain; at 2045 base assumptions the net proceeds are about $5,655,210 after a 5% selling-cost band and the 0.11% county transfer tax. Every hold path must beat this number, with its risks, to be chosen.
  2. Property tax is the swing cost, and Proposition 19 decides it. Without the family-home exclusion, a 2045 transfer re-assesses the home to about $5,959,750 and the tax bill rises roughly eight-fold — about $79,260 per year versus about $10,670 with the exclusion. Claim the exclusion only if the heir will genuinely occupy the home long term.
  3. Letting is a floor, not a headline. Comparable asking rents of $5,150–$8,400 per month for similar houses give an evidence band; net rent after the actual tax bill, insurance and reserves is materially lower. The rent path ranks on net figures in Section 05.
  4. Staged investing is crash insurance with a visible premium. The historical replay shows phasing into 1929-type entries is worth up to +107.9 points at ten years, but the median ten-year window costs −74.9 points versus immediate investing; it wins in 15.7% of windows. Reinvestment risk sits next to every staging headline.
  5. Estate tax is a real, separate question. A 2026 death has a $15,000,000 federal basic exclusion (then 40% above it); California has no estate return under current law. A "considerable" cash inheritance inside a larger estate can still push the estate over the line — the liquidity worksheet in Section 08 must be completed now, and portability/prior taxable gifts asked about now.
  6. Nothing here is decided. The pre-commitment criteria (Section 12) are meant to be signed before figures arrive. The model is re-run every year; the decision log (Section 13) records what changed and when.

06 · PROPOSITION 19

The exclusion, the allowance, and the honest conditions

Claim the parent-child exclusion only if the heir will occupy the home long term. Everything else is a fraud risk and an assessment risk.

  • Eligibility: qualifying family transfer (parent-child / limited grandparent-grandchild), the transferor's home was their principal residence, and the transferee occupies it as their principal residence. BOE Prop 19 · RTC 63.2
  • Deadlines: homeowners' exemption filed within one year of transfer; exclusion claim within three years or before a third-party transfer, whichever is earlier. BOE guidance
  • Value cap: the transferee keeps the factored base-year value only up to that value plus the adjusted $1,000,000 allowance. Current allowance: $1,044,590 for transfers 16 Feb 2025 – 15 Feb 2027 (prior window $1,022,600). BOE LTA 2025/009
  • Allowance carried forward (placeholder): adjustments happen every second February 16 against the FHFA California house price index. The observed step between the last two published windows is +2.15%. At that step, the allowance would be ≈ $1,212,310 (2040) and ≈ $1,265,000 (2045) — placeholder only; future adjustments are unknown and no future allowance is law.
