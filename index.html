A private comparison framework for an inherited home, an already-owned multi-unit home, and other inherited assets. It keeps the existing San Francisco property separate from the Property A inheritance and does not pretend the current facts settle a 2040–2050 decision.

!
No defensible winner yet

Tax basis, eligibility, transfer-year law, rents, insurance, capital needs, and estate liquidity are not verified. A path recommendation now would be false precision.

What would change the answer?

  1. Eligibility and occupancy: Is the recipient an eligible child, and will the inherited home genuinely become their long-term primary residence within the applicable deadline?
  2. Transfer-date economics: Date-of-death valuation, market rent, transaction costs, taxes, and the new assessed value determine the hold-versus-sell baseline.
  3. Household fit: What net rent and obligations can the already-owned three-unit home support, and what value does living in the inherited home add?

How the illustrative arithmetic works

Retained property uses the chosen home growth rate plus annual imputed-rent or net-rent benefits compounded as if reinvested. The annual net benefit subtracts entered inherited-home tax, insurance, utilities, maintenance and capital-reserve inputs; a single selected inflation rate escalates all net cash flows, which is not a substitute for Prop 13, insurance-renewal or utility forecasts. Sold proceeds and liquid assets grow at the selected portfolio return. A phased path applies the selected Treasury yield to uninvested proceeds during the 24–36-month phase-in, then the selected equity return; the yield is not fixed in advance. The optional shock is applied once to invested balances in the selected year. The existing SF property's market-value range and baseline net cash flow are included in every path; the entered SF occupancy cash-flow change is added only to the Property A live-in path. Inflation adjustment divides terminal nominal values by (1 + inflation)years. The engine does not calculate federal/state income tax, estate tax, Prop 19 / property-tax reassessment, depreciation or recapture, rent-control limits, transaction fees, debt, insurance quote changes, distribution timing, or loss aversion. The FTB 2025 surtax sensitivity is separate and does not alter proceeds. Supply after-tax estimates and consult a CPA/attorney; never treat the output as a final answer.

03 · REGIME & CRASH TESTS

Black swans: test the entry sequence, not a headline

Long periods do not deliver a smooth average. The critical comparison is the same transfer date, spending needs, and horizon under different paths for returns, inflation and rates. See the cited public-market anchors for grounded stress reference data.

A · FRONT-LOADED CRASH

Crash in year 1

Apply a user-selected equity drawdown immediately after transfer. Compare all-at-once investment with 24- and 36-month Treasury phase-ins. A staggered entry can reduce exposure to an immediate drop, but leaves uninvested proceeds exposed to inflation and changing reinvestment yields.

Reinvestment risk: explicit
B · INFLATION / RATE SHOCK

Purchasing-power squeeze

Raise inflation, reset short-Treasury yields after each maturity, and increase property insurance, utilities, taxes and capital costs by separate rates. Fixed nominal cash can lose purchasing power; property and stocks are not guaranteed inflation hedges.

Rate & cost risk: separate inputs
C · STAGFLATION / SLOW RECOVERY

Weak returns, high costs

Test several years of low or negative real returns while repairs, insurance and vacancies rise. Compare liquidity runway and ability to fund both properties without forced sales. Recheck debt, estate cash and capital needs.

Liquidity risk: annual review
D · HOUSING / RENT SHOCK

Flat values, rent disruption

Stress lower sale value and rent, longer vacancy, lease restrictions, major repairs, and a higher assessed bill. Obtain unit-level rental comparables and confirm every lease and local restriction before treating gross rent as investable cash.

05 · DEADLINES & PROCESS

Inheritance process, in sequence

These are planning checkpoints, not a substitute for the trust instrument, court notice, assessor notice, or tax adviser’s calendar. Exact due dates depend on the transfer method and then-current law.

  1. Before transfer · now to event

    Agree decision rules in writing

    Household states what facts would favor living, renting, selling, or a mix. Decide long-term occupancy intent; establish private inventory and investment policy; ask about portability and prior taxable gifts.

    Owner: household + estate attorney / CPA
  2. At death / transfer date

    Secure authority, title, cash and valuations

    Trustee/executor confirms governing documents, title, debt, beneficiaries, date-of-death valuation, basis records, insurance and continuity of utilities/leases. Do not publicly disclose the asset inventory.

    Owner: trustee / executor
  3. Promptly · claim clocks matter

    Check Prop 19 eligibility and file on time

    For a qualifying family-home transfer, verify relationship and both parties' principal-residence status. BOE guidance says the transferee must establish principal residence and file for the homeowners' exemption within one year; file the exclusion claim within three years or before a third-party transfer, whichever is earlier. A late claim may have a six-month window after a supplemental / escape assessment notice. Use the official BOE guidance and transfer-year form.

    Owner: heir + county assessor · deadline: confirm from notice / BOE
  4. Before any sale

    Get tax and transaction estimates

    CPA models same-tax-year sale, basis, capital gain, depreciation and exclusions; title/escrow prices transfer / recording charges. Discuss a §1031 exchange before disposition only if property qualifies as business/investment use; a personal-use residence cannot simply be exchanged.

    Owner: CPA + exchange counsel + escrow
  5. Tax return deadlines · year of death

    File only the required federal and state returns

    Executor checks Form 706 threshold, adjusted taxable gifts and filing deadlines; a portability election may justify a Form 706 even below threshold. Gift/income tax, fiduciary income returns, property claims and notices have separate rules.

    Federal Form 706 generally due 9 months after death; verify current instructions
  6. First year after transfer

    Stabilize occupancy, insurance, tax basis and leases

    Confirm recorded title and assessor record, review supplemental assessment/appeal rights, document home use, update landlord/tenant compliance, validate coverage, schedule repairs and protect estate liquidity.

    Owner: heir / trustee + advisers
  7. Annually · repeat

    Re-run the model and law watch

    Refresh quotes, rents, condition, assessed values, cash flows, legislation and investment assumptions. Record decision, date, source, chosen path and why it remains suitable.

    Review at least yearly and after a major law / household change

Federal Form 706 and Prop 19 deadlines must be checked against official instructions and actual notices at the time. A decision modeled for 2040–2050 cannot use today's legal thresholds as guaranteed future law.

09 · GLOSSARY

Terms that should not be mixed up

Definitions are decision aids; governing statutes, regulations and tax forms control.

Estate tax
Federal tax assessed on an estate, subject to the exclusion, deductions, adjusted taxable gifts and filing rules. It is not a tax charged automatically on each inheritance.
Income-tax basis
Starting value for gain/loss calculations. Inherited-property basis is generally tied to fair market value at death, with important exceptions and reporting rules.
Assessed value / factored base-year value
California property-tax value, generally constrained by Proposition 13 until a reassessment or qualifying exclusion applies. Not the same as current market value.
Prop 19 intergenerational exclusion
Limited California property-tax reassessment exclusion for qualifying family homes/farms and eligible transferees, subject to occupancy, value and filing requirements.
Imputed rent
Estimated value of housing services gained by living in an owned home, net of costs. It is an economic comparison input, not rent income received.
Depreciation / recapture
Rental deductions reduce tax basis; depreciation allowed or allowable may change taxable gain or recapture on sale.
Reinvestment risk
Risk that principal from maturing short-term securities must be reinvested at lower yields. A phased investment plan can also leave cash underinvested during a rising market.
Market rent
Evidence-based estimate of rent for comparable lawful units and terms—not a voucher payment standard or an unverified asking price.
Mixed path
Retain and use/rent a selected share while selling/investing another share or changing one property's use independently.
Tax rate area (TRA)
Geographic unit with a common property-tax rate composition. Parcel's actual rate may include voter-approved debt and assessments; citywide averages cannot identify it.

Attorney & CPA Briefing Sheet

Objective: compare living in, renting, selling, staged reinvestment, and mixed paths for an inherited California home while modeling an already-owned multi-unit property separately.

Known / distinction

  • One home is expected to transfer by trust in a future period; target date is uncertain (2040–2050 planning window).
  • A separate San Francisco property is already owned by the prospective heir and has multi-unit rental/occupancy use; its title, leases and net cash flow require verification.
  • Private property details and submitted estimates are withheld from this sheet and public site.

Questions for attorney

  • Who is transferor/beneficiary; does current Prop 19 definition apply? Was transferor's home a principal residence?
  • What transfer date controls; what are the one-year occupancy / filing deadlines? Can the heir genuinely occupy long term?
  • Trust administration, title, debt, leases, legal unit count, transfer taxes / fees and any sale or exchange constraints?
  • Is federal estate tax return required; should portability be elected based on spouse status and prior gifts?

Questions for CPA / tax adviser

  • Document date-of-death FMV basis and consistent-basis statement; model immediate same-year sale.
  • Compute federal/California sale tax, primary-home exclusion eligibility, depreciation and recapture; verify capital gain and additional 1% CA tax.
  • Estimate reassessed property tax by actual parcel / TRA, Prop 19 outcome and special assessments.
  • Is an exchange wanted and eligible before sale? What estate liquidity / tax payments are required?

Model inputs to gather

  • Independent appraisal & rental comps; legal leases / permits; tax bills; five-year inspections and bids.
  • Written insurance quotations; utility history; actual debt; liquidity and portfolio spending policy.
  • Shared transfer date and 5/10/20/30-year horizons; low/base/high assumptions and historical crash dataset.
