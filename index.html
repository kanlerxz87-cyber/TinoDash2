Executive summary · decision readiness

Start with the facts that could change the answer.

One inherited California home is not the same decision as an already-owned home with rentals. The options cannot be ranked responsibly until inheritance timing, tax basis, reassessment, occupancy, local rental rules, actual costs and after-tax sale proceeds are verified.

No single-path recommendation yet
What would change the answer: (1) whether the inherited home qualifies for Proposition 19 and whether the heir will actually occupy it as a principal residence within one year; (2) date-of-death value and estate liquidity/tax facts; (3) verified Cupertino tax-rate-area bill, insurance and capital plan; (4) actual rent/expenses/leases and allowed occupancy changes for the existing multi-unit home; (5) sale-year, basis, depreciation and income-tax facts; and (6) written household priorities. Compare these before using return projections.

Not decision-ready
Live in inherited home

Could preserve an eligible Proposition 19 base value only if the statutory tests are met, including timely principal-residence occupancy; do not claim the exclusion unless intending to occupy long term. Living there also has imputed rent/opportunity cost and changes where the household lives.

Rent inherited home

May create income, but immediately fails the heir-occupancy requirement for Prop 19 if the heir does not live there as required. Market rent, landlord costs, insurance, vacancy and local compliance are unverified; the property-tax reassessment may dominate the economics.

Sell / invest / mixed

May release liquidity and diversify, but sale timing, stepped-up basis, gain, costs, tax, reinvestment and sequencing all matter. Mixed paths (live then sell, rent then sell, retain one property and sell the other) must be compared on the same date and horizon.

Current-law anchor: federal estate exclusion

$15,000,000
Federal basic estate-tax exclusion for a decedent dying in 2026, before considering adjusted taxable gifts and other applicable exclusion components.
Official IRS 2026 publication · not a 2040–2050 forecast

Estate tax is assessed on the taxable estate—not simply “inheritance received” by an individual. Trust title, ownership, prior gifts, deductions and portability can alter the calculation. Future limits are unknown. California State Controller guidance says no California estate-tax return is required for decedents dying after 2004 under current law; other-state and property/transaction obligations are separate.

Current-law anchor: Prop 19 transfer test

For eligible parent/child (and limited grandparent/grandchild) transfers, the family home must generally have been the transferor’s principal residence; the recipient must make it their principal residence within one year and claim the homeowners’ or disabled-veterans’ exemption. Eligibility is not established by the information available here.

For transfers dated Feb. 16, 2025–Feb. 15, 2027, BOE publishes an adjusted exclusion allowance of $1,044,586 added to the transferor’s factored base-year value. That period-specific allowance is not a forecast for an inheritance expected around 2040–2050.

Existing San Francisco home changes the comparison

Reported arrangement (not independently verified): another already-owned San Francisco property has two rented units and a third unit that may be available for owner occupancy. The inherited house should not be evaluated in isolation: keeping the existing rent stream while living in a separate vacant unit could be a mixed/occupancy choice, subject to leases, code, rent/eviction rules, insurance and actual property rights.

Do not infer that a unit may lawfully be occupied, that tenants can be moved, or that rent will continue unchanged. See the SF rental-law overview ↗.
