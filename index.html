FIRST SCREEN · DECISION GATES

What would change the answer?

Agreed in writing before the figures arrived, so the outcome is not reverse-engineered from one projection.

  1. Prop 19 eligibility and genuine occupancy. If Owner B is an eligible transferee, will occupy Property A as a principal residence, and files the homeowners' exemption within 1 year and the claim within 3 years, the live-in path keeps a much lower assessed value. Without occupancy, no exclusion applies and the rent/hold paths carry a full reassessment. Rules and math
  2. Real rent evidence for Property A. The rent path is currently ranked against the HUD 3-bedroom county benchmark (an official floor) plus an unverified planning range. Verified comparable rents above roughly $7,200/month (2045 terms) would need to appear before renting beats selling. Rent evidence status
  3. Estate size versus the federal exclusion. Property A + Property B alone stay far below the projected exclusion at every modeled transfer year; only the "other inherited cash" can push the estate over. If it does, liquidity and portability decisions become urgent. Estate analysis
  4. Date-of-death valuation and sale timing. A sale in the same tax year as inheritance realizes approximately zero gain (stepped-up basis). Delaying sale accrues future gain, future taxes, and carrying costs. Basis rules
  5. Insurance, condition, and capital needs. A five-year capital plan for each building can flip a paper-positive hold to negative. Quotes are not yet obtained. Five-year capital plans
  6. Black swan entry timing. A 1929/2008-style crash in the first 24–36 months after investing is the scenario the phased-investing path is built for. Crash-entry tests

Why the sell paths lead in the base case

  • The step-up makes selling cheap now. A same-tax-year sale realizes approximately $0 gain. IRS basis rules
  • Reassessment makes keeping expensive. Even with Prop 19, Property A's tax jumps from $7,210 (2026) to roughly $54,700/yr at a 2045 transfer ($69,480 if rented, no exclusion). Prop 19 math
  • Market rent is thin versus a $6M asset. Projected 2045 gross rent ≈ $10,350/mo (benchmark-derived) versus ≈ $5,480/mo of property tax alone. Rent evidence
  • Phasing is cheap insurance. Parking at ~4.5% for 24–36 months costs 4.5–6.6% of terminal value if no crash comes, but beats lump-sum by $12.7M–$30.5M in a year-1 1929/2008-style crash. Test table
