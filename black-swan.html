Why a separate black-swan section

Growth and inflation are never consistent over 50 years. A 50-year average hides the 1929s, 1970s, 2000s and 2008s that decide real outcomes.

03 · BLACK SWAN SECTION · SEPARATE FROM BASELINE PROJECTIONS

Black swans: test the entry sequence, not a headline

This section models how the sale-and-reinvest decision behaves when the entry lands on a crisis — and quantifies the owner-requested phased path: parking proceeds in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries for 24–36 months, then phasing into stocks.

Real regimes from history (public anchors, labeled by index basis)

A · 1929-STYLE DEFLATION

Depression entry

Equities fell ≈89% peak-to-trough (DJIA price basis, Sep 1929–Jul 1932) while CPI fell ≈25% (1930–32). Cash/Treasuries gained real purchasing power; nominal debts crushed borrowers.

FRED DJIA · NBER
B · 1970s STAGFLATION

Inflation squeeze

Equities fell ≈48% (S&P 500 price basis, 1973–74) with CPI averaging 11.3–13.5% (1979–81). Short Treasuries lost real value most years — parking is not safe from inflation.

FRED SP500 · BLS CPI
C · 2000-2002 DOT-COM

Long grind

≈49% decline over 2.5 years (S&P 500 price basis) with the Fed cutting 6.5%→1.0%; parking yields collapsed as the crisis ran — reinvestment risk in action.

FRED SP500 · Fed actions
D · 2008 GFC

Both assets fall

Equities ≈−57% (S&P 500 price basis); national home prices ≈−27% (public index anchor). The 3-month T-bill yield collapsed from ≈5% to near zero — each maturity reinvested lower.

FRED SP500/DTB3 · Fed history
E · 2022 RATE SHOCK

Stocks AND bonds fall

Equities ≈−25% while Treasuries had one of their worst years in decades with CPI at 8.0% — proof that the parking leg itself carries rate risk, not just equity risk.

FRED SP500 · Treasury rates
F · LOST DECADE / STAGNATION

Years of nothing

S&P 500 total-return CAGR ≈−1%/yr for 2000–2009; Japan's Nikkei spent decades below its 1989 peak. Averages lie: whole decades can pass without real progress.

FRED SP500/NIKKEI225
