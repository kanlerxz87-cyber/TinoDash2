Black swan entry tests

The decision that dominates every other modeling detail is the entry sequence: selling an inherited property and redeploying proceeds into risk assets at the top of a euphoric market versus being already invested through it. A staging plan (3-month–2-year Treasuries for 24–36 months, then equities) is insurance, not a return source — in a bull market it is a cost, and that cost should be admitted out loud before it is chosen. The replays below price both sides of that trade on one published dataset.

1 · The phased-investing test — $1,000,000 at each historical entry date

Same starting money, three deployment plans: immediate (all at once), 24-month (equal tranches at months 0/12/24, parked in 3-month T-bills until each tranche deploys), 36-month (months 0/12/24/36). Returns: S&P 500 annual total return and average 3-month T-bill rate (Damodaran histretSP, Jan 2026 dataset).

Read the two-sided result: in 1929, 1973, 2008 and 2022 entries, staging won — sometimes overwhelmingly (1929 at 4 years: ≈$352,300 immediate vs ≈$577,530 staged). In the 2013 bull entry, staging lost clearly (≈$1,321,500 immediate vs ≈$1,107,570 staged after 1 year). No plan wins both cases; the point of the pre-agreed investment policy is to pick your premium in advance.

2 · Replay rules (so the numbers are reproducible)

  1. Entry at the start of the named calendar year; $1,000,000 split into equal tranches per plan.
  2. Each parked tranche earns that year's average 3-month T-bill rate until its deployment year; from then it earns that year's S&P 500 total return.
  3. Values are nominal, rounded to $10, before tax and fees. Total-return basis — do not compare with the price-only anchors in section 3 without relabelling.
  4. Source: NYU Stern (Damodaran) — Historical Returns on Stocks, Bonds and Bills (Jan 2026).

6 · Decision tests, stated before the numbers arrive

  1. Write the pre-commitment first. Pick the staging rule, the tranche schedule, the acceleration trigger (e.g., a large market fall with a written percentage) and the “enough” end state — in writing, dated — before any appraisal or sale closes.
  2. Liquidity runway first. Stage only what is not needed for near-term housing, tax payments, or the San Francisco property’s obligations; staging is for risk capital, not for the mortgage.
  3. Same dates, same horizons. Compare immediate investing, 24-month staging and 36-month staging on identical transfer dates, taxes and horizons — never on different calendars.
  4. Admission of the base rate. Most calendar years are positive; staging usually trails prompt deployment in a bull year (2013 replay). Choose it knowing that, or choose the lump sum knowing the 1929/2008 case.
  5. Annual Extreme-Scenario Review. Once a year, replay the plan against the 1929-, 1973-, 2008-, 2013- and 2022-type anchors above, plus one household-chosen stress size. Record the outcome in the decision log.
