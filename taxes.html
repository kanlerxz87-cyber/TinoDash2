Taxes & law — three taxes, kept separate

Estate tax, income-tax basis, and property-tax assessment are different systems with different thresholds, deadlines, and planning levers. Conflating them is the most common inheritance error. Current law as of Nov 2026; future law (2040s) is projected with sensitivities. Enacted changes are distinguished from proposals below.

1 · Estate tax (federal only)

Exemption 2026: $15,000,000/person ($30M/couple with portability). Permanent under OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, July 4 2025); indexed from 2027. Top rate 40% above exemption. Official — IRS

California: $0 — no estate tax, no inheritance tax. Official — FTB/CA law

Combined A ($3.1M) + B ($1.8M) = $4.9M (2026) is well below $15M. Even with 3% growth to 2045 (~$8.4M), still below — unless other assets (cash inheritance, investments) push the gross estate over. Inventory the full estate (Q-02). If over, prepare liquidity plan (Q-11).

2 · Income-tax basis (step-up)

IRC §1014: basis = fair market value at date of death. Pre-death appreciation is never income-taxed. Sell same year ≈ $0 gain. Official — IRS Pub 544

IRC §121: $250k single / $500k joint exclusion if owned + lived 2 of 5 yrs. Heir's own use counts; decedent's does not. Official — IRS

Depreciation (rent path): 27.5-yr straight-line; unrecaptured §1250 ≤25% on sale. CA: gains taxed as ordinary income (no preferential rate).

3 · Property-tax assessment (Prop 13/19)

Prop 13: 1% + voter bonds; factored base +≤2%/yr. Official — BOE Pub 29

Prop 19 parent-child: family home only; heir must occupy within 1 yr; cap = factored base + $1,044,586 (2025–27, indexed biennially). Rentals/second homes: no exclusion. Official — BOE

Allowance carries forward via factoring (2%/yr) once established. Claim: BOE-19-P within 3 yrs (before 3rd-party transfer); homeowners' exemption within 1 yr.

Form 540 — 1% tax above $1M (Mental Health / Behavioral Health Services Tax)

Rule (R&TC §17043): additional 1% on California taxable income above $1,000,000 (not on the first $1M). Reported on Form 540 line 62 (2025+). Threshold never indexed. Official — FTB Form 540 instructions

Capital-gain treatment: CA has no preferential capital-gains rate — gains are ordinary income (1%–12.3%, +1% MHST = 13.3% top). A large sale year can push taxable income over $1M and trigger the 1% on the excess. Official — FTB

Model sensitivity (updated per instructions): Dashboard sale-tax uses a simplified 9.3% CA rate. True liability depends on filing status, other income, and whether the gain stacks over $1M. Example (illustrative, rounded): $500,000 gain stacking on $700,000 other income → $1.2M taxable → 1% × $200,000 = $2,000 MHST, plus regular CA tax on the full gain at marginal rates. Spreading the gain can avoid the surtax entirely.

Prop H (San Francisco Rent Ordinance) — official terms

Ambiguity flagged (F-01): "Prop H" matches multiple SF measures. Site models the SF Rent Ordinance Prop H provisions below. Confirm which Prop H the household means before relying on this section.

Prop H (2000) — capital-improvement passthrough limitation: prohibits most capital-improvement rent increases unless necessary for constitutionally required fair return. Affects B's ability to pass through capex to tenants. Official — SF Rent Board archive

Prop H (2006) — relocation payments: $4,500/occupant (max $13,500/unit) + $3,000 elderly/disabled/minor-child add-on, inflation-adjusted annually, for owner/relative move-in, demolition, capital-improvement, and substantial-rehab evictions. Official — SF Rent Board archive

Model impact: B's rent growth and capex recovery are constrained; owner move-in (if heir moves to B instead of A) triggers relocation payments. Verify B's coverage status (pre-1979? TIC? condo?) — Q-09.

Transfer tax & recording fees (sell path)

County documentary transfer tax: $1.10 per $1,000 of sale price (R&TC §11911), typically seller-paid. On $3.1M ≈ $3,410. Official rate — CA law

City transfer tax: Cupertino — verify (no LA-style tier assumed). SF has its own schedule if B were sold. Verify city rate — F-02

Recording: ~$15 first page + per-page fees + $75 SB-2 fee on many non-sale transfers; PCOR (BOE-502-A) required. Official — BOE/county

Model bundles all sell costs at 6.5% (commission-dominated). Transfer/recording alone are <0.2% — commissions drive the cost. Get a net sheet from a broker before deciding.
